What stands out Centers and power forwards have the highest average career counts in this dataset. LeBron James leads the recorded list with 23 seasons through 2026.

Answer: The average player appears in about 4.7 seasons, while the median is 3. The distribution is uneven: many players appear for only one or a few seasons, while a small group sustains careers for 15–20+ seasons. Position differences are descriptive, not proof that position itself causes longer careers.

When do players peak?

PER and BPM offer two different lenses on performance. The averages below compare player-seasons by age band.

Answer: In this dataset, average PER is highest in the 28–30 age band. Performance is lower on average among the youngest groups, rises through the mid-to-late twenties, and generally declines after age 30. That pattern is an average, not a rule for every player. Karl Malone and LeBron James are examples of players who posted outstanding advanced metrics after age 35.

How to read this PER is a per-minute individual productivity metric. BPM estimates a player's point impact per 100 possessions relative to league average. Neither is a direct measure of points, assists, or rebounds.
03 Three-point evolution

The arc of the three.

The dataset records three-point attempt rate (3PAr), the share of field-goal attempts that are three-pointers—not raw attempts per game.

Three-point attempts as a share of all field-goal attempts

Minutes-weighted average 3PAr by season · NBA · 1980–2026

Answer: The three-point attempt share rises dramatically over the period. It moves from about 2.9% in 1980 to 41.2% in 2026. This supports the conclusion that the three-point shot became a much larger part of NBA shot selection. Because this file does not include individual 3-point attempt totals, it cannot identify who made the most threes or quantify a player's exact contribution to the league-wide increase.

The positional pattern Centers lead in rebound percentage and PER; guards lead in assist percentage. Forwards sit between those groups in rebounding and playmaking rates.

Answer: The data reflects familiar role differences. Centers have the highest average rebound percentage, while guards have the highest average assist percentage. Centers also show the highest average PER in this grouping. BPM is relatively close across groups and is negative on average for all three, which reflects the inclusion of every player-season—not just stars or starters.

PER vs. BPM PER emphasizes individual box-score productivity and efficiency. BPM estimates overall per-possession impact, including offense and defense. The leaders overlap, but the rankings differ—one reason efficiency is better understood with multiple metrics rather than a single number.

Answer: Nikola Jokić, Michael Jordan, and LeBron James rank near the top in both career-weighted measures among players meeting the minutes threshold. PER and BPM tell related but not identical stories. VORP is also included in the source and can represent cumulative value above a replacement-level player, but the career leader tables here use PER and BPM for easier comparison.

Methodology & limitations

What the data can—and can't—say.

Source: The uploaded Advanced.csv file. The file contains NBA, ABA, and BAA rows; this project filters to rows labeled NBA and seasons through 2026. The 2027 season is excluded because it is in progress.

Traded players: Where the file provides a 2TM combined row for a player-season, that aggregate is used instead of the separate team rows. Where no combined row exists, team stints are combined; rate statistics are weighted by minutes to avoid double-counting a traded player.

Career length: Count of distinct seasons with a player record in this dataset. It is not necessarily a complete measure of time spent under contract or years between debut and retirement.

Three-point trend: 3PAr is the proportion of field-goal attempts that are three-point attempts. It is not 3-point attempts per game. The chart starts in 1980, when the three-point line was introduced in the NBA.

Limitations: The file does not include traditional points, assists, rebounds, salary, All-Star selections, nationality, or raw three-point attempts. Therefore, the project does not make claims about scoring leaders, salary value, All-Star correlations, international representation, or traditional per-game averages. Results are descriptive and do not establish causation.
